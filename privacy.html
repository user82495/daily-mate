DailyMate

Privacy

The short version

Daily Mate stores a random number on your device and records, once a day, whether you solved that day's puzzle, how many attempts it took, and how long it took. If you turn on reminders it also keeps the hour you chose and your time zone. There are no accounts, no personal data, and no third-party analytics of any kind.

What is collected

When a puzzle ends — solved or not — the app sends exactly four values, once, and never again for that day:

Anonymous device ID
A random UUID your browser generates for itself and keeps in localStorage. It is not derived from anything about you or your device, it is not shared with anyone, and it means nothing outside Daily Mate's own database. It is created the first time you finish a puzzle — if you open Daily Mate and never play, no ID is ever made.
Puzzle day
Which day's puzzle it was, as a calendar date.
Result
Solved, or not solved.
Attempts used
One, two, or three.
Solve time
How long the puzzle took, in seconds, measured from the board appearing to your last move. It is what the “faster than 78% of solvers” line is worked out from. It is not a timer on you: nothing is stored about when you played, only how long it took.

That is the complete list for playing. It exists so that one person can see how many people are playing, which puzzles turn out to be too hard, and so the app can show you your own streak.

If you turn on reminders

Notifications are off unless you ask for them, and you are only ever asked after you have finished a puzzle — never when you arrive. If you say yes, two more things are stored alongside your anonymous ID:

The hour you picked
A number from 0 to 23. Nothing else about your schedule.
Your time zone
The name your browser reports, such as Europe/London. This is the only way to send a reminder at 9am your time from a job that runs once an hour. It is a zone, not a location — it is the same string for everyone in that zone.

Turning notifications off in your browser or clearing site data ends it. A reminder that can no longer be delivered has its record deleted automatically the next time one is attempted.

What is not collected

  • No accounts, names, email addresses, or sign-in of any kind.
  • No IP addresses. They are not logged and are never stored.
  • No user agent, device model, screen size, or browser fingerprint.
  • No location, at any precision, including country.
  • No cookies. Nothing Daily Mate stores is sent as a cookie.
  • No page views, timings, scroll or click tracking, or session recording.
  • No record of your individual moves.
  • No third-party analytics, advertising, tag managers, or embedded trackers. Daily Mate loads no code from anyone else's server.
  • No cross-site tracking, and no way to do it: the anonymous ID exists on this site only and follows you nowhere.

Your stats stay on your device

Played, solved percentage, and the attempts histogram are computed and stored in your browser, under the localStorage key dailymate.v1. The anonymous ID sits beside it under dailymate.anon.v1, mirrored to dm_player_id.

One thing changed when streaks moved to the server: your streak is now counted there too, against your anonymous ID, so that it survives and cannot be edited from the page. The stats page shows the server's figures when it can reach them and your device's own when it cannot.

Clearing your browser's site data for Daily Mate erases both: your stats and streak are gone, and your next completed puzzle mints a brand new anonymous ID with no link to the old one.

The one number that comes back

The stats screen can show a line like "63% of players solved today's puzzle." That is a single aggregate figure, and it only appears once at least twenty people have played that day. Asking for it sends no identifier — not even the anonymous ID.

Where it is kept

The four values above are stored in a Supabase database, reachable only by the site's own server. Your browser never talks to it directly, which is also why no request of yours is logged there. The site is hosted on Netlify, which necessarily handles your request in order to serve the page; Daily Mate does not ask it for, use, or retain anything about it.

Data requests

There is nothing to look up. A record here is a random number and a result, with no way to connect it to a person — including by the person who runs Daily Mate. If you want your device's record gone, clear the site's data in your browser: the ID it was filed under stops existing, and what remains is an anonymous row that identifies nobody.

Changes

If what is collected ever changes, this page changes with it. Daily Mate is free software and unminified, so the code that does the sending is readable in full — it is the file js/analytics.js, and it is short.
